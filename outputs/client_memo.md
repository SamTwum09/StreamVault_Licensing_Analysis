### 1. Executive Recommendation: Prioritise Franchise Bundles
Commit the majority of the $14.0M capital envelope to **franchise bundles**, while deploying a disciplined allocation to **standalone hits** as tactical complements.

* **The Evidence:** Evaluating candidate films from 2000 onward, franchise entries generated a median historical box-office-to-budget multiple of **5.05x** (and **5.62x** across all years), versus **3.84x** for standalone films in both periods.Franchise candidates showed stronger historical box-office performance relative to reported production budgets in this sample. Their multi-title structure also offers a potential catalog and marketing advantage, although streaming retention and licensing returns were not measured.
* **The Window Comparison (Addressing Finance):** Finance’s concern regarding pre-2000 reporting anomalies is warranted. When restricting the portfolio to 2000-onward releases, the franchise multiple moderates slightly from 5.62x to 5.05x, but **the commercial advantage remains decisive (+31% multiple advantage)**. The recommendation does not change between windows.

### 3. Talent as a Negotiating Lever: Directors & Lead Actors
Negotiators should use historical talent performance as leverage when studios justify license fees:

* **Directors Signaling Outperformance:** **Anthony & Joe Russo** (7.55x median multiple, $5.0B revenue) and **Peter Jackson** (6.73x median multiple across 6 films, $5.85B revenue) reliably anchor high-efficiency blockbusters. Use their presence to justify premium bundle pricing, and conversely demand discounts when studios package franchise sequels directed by replacement directors.
* **Lead Actors with Proven Pull:** Lead actors associated with strong historical performance in this sample include Chris Evans, Mark Ruffalo, Elijah Wood and Viggo Mortensen. These results are descriptive associations within the candidate pool and should not be interpreted as isolated evidence of an actor's ability to drive streaming demand.

---

### 4. Deal Note: The Single Strongest Candidate (The Avengers Collection)
* **What to Pay Relative to Everything Else:** Authorize a firm ceiling of **35% to 40% of the total budget ($5.0M–$5.6M)** for The Avengers Collection. Paying more than $5.6M crowds out necessary standalone acquisitions and leaves the catalog overexposed to a single studio. If Disney insists on pricing the bundle above $6.0M, walk away and pivot to *The Lord of the Rings* ($3.0M cap) plus *Harry Potter*.
* **The One Number Driving This View:** **$7.92 Billion** in cumulative historical box office across just 4 titles. No other candidate bundle combines this sheer volume of proven audience demand with a 6.87x median multiple.

### 6. Improvement Beyond the Brief: The $14M Capital Allocation Framework
Rather than treating $14.0M as an unallocated spending pool, we establish an evidence-based capital structure:
* **Franchise Anchor Tier ($8.0M / 57%):** Cap Avengers at $5.0M; allocate $3.0M to Lord of the Rings.
* **Standalone Tactical Tier ($3.5M / 25%):** Secure Inception, Interstellar, and Spirited Away.
* **Contingency & Rights Reserve ($2.5M / 18%):** Held to absorb mandatory studio bundle add-ons, music rights clearances, or secondary standalone pickups.
* **Value to Kwame:** Gives the Board an airtight capital deployment defense, preventing last-minute budget reallocations to original productions.
